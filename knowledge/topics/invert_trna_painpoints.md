# Invertebrate mitochondrial tRNA annotation: pain points and existing methods

Scope: what makes mt-tRNA genes hard to annotate in invertebrates (armless/truncated shapes, editing, overlaps, losses, rearrangement), quantitative evidence of how existing tools fail, newer methods, and how benchmark truth is built. Does not repeat ml_trna_prediction.md (ML/DL tools, training-data inventory) or trna_naming_anticodon.md (L1/L2/S1/S2 naming, anticodon qualifiers, missing-tRNA clades, overlap counts); where a fact already lives there it is only cross-referenced. Every bullet carries a DOI/URL or the marker 未确证. Evidence came from Europe PMC REST (full-text XML where open access), PubMed Central and publisher abstracts, read 2026-10-26. 推测 = my inference from cited facts, not a published statement.

Citation correction: the brief names "Jühling et al. 2012 Genome Biol Evol, armless mitochondrial tRNAs in Enoplea". The paper with that title is Jühling F, Pütz J, Florentz C, Stadler PF (2012) RNA Biology 9(9):1161-1166, doi:10.4161/rna.21630 (PMC3579883), not Genome Biol Evol. The Genome Biol Evol mt-tRNA paper in this area is Ozerova et al. 2024 (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232).

## 1. Armless and truncated tRNAs: which clade loses which arm (not repeating the clade-level percentages already in trna_naming_anticodon.md)

### Nematoda: Chromadorea versus Enoplea
- Origin of the "bizarre tRNA" concept: Ascaris suum mtDNA has no standard-form tRNA genes; 21 sequences fold into tRNA-like structures lacking the TΨC arm and the variable loop, replaced by a single loop of 4-12 nt; C. elegans has the same type (doi:10.1073/pnas.84.5.1324) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC304420/)
- Chromadorea rule: 20 of 22 mt-tRNAs are T-armless (D-arm kept, "TV-replacement loop") and the two serines are D-armless (T-arm kept); each tRNA loses exactly one arm (doi:10.4161/rna.21630) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3579883/)
- Enoplea / Trichinella spiralis (Trichocephalida): 8 cloverleaf tRNAs, 12 T-armless, 2 D-armless serines (Jühling 2012); MITOS2-based reannotation lists 7 cloverleaf in Trichinellida (Ile, Lys, Leu1, Leu2, Met, Arg, Trp) - the two counts differ by one, so the number depends on method (doi:10.4161/rna.21630) (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)
- Enoplea / Dorylaimida + Mermithida with nematode-specific CMs: all retrieved tRNAs are abnormal, 9% lack the D-arm, 56% the T-arm, 35% both; Trichinella is the only Enoplea with a mixed cloverleaf set (doi:10.4161/rna.21630)
- Family-level pattern in Mermithida: tRNA-Ala lost the D-stem in all Mermithida; D-stem of Trp preserved in most species (reduced in two Romanomermis sequences, NC_008640 and NC_008693); Ala, His, Tyr appear only as acceptor-stem + anticodon-stem fragments in the older analysis (doi:10.4161/rna.21630)
- Xiphinema americanum (Dorylaimida): published annotation had 19 of 22 tRNAs (Cys, Asn, Ser(UGA) "lacking"), three of them D-armless; nematode-specific CMs recovered candidates for all 22 without postulating overlapping opposite-strand tRNAs (doi:10.4161/rna.21630)
- Armless length: shortest known mt-tRNA is 45 nt including CCA in Romanomermis culicivorax (acceptor stem + anticodon stem only); in-vitro transcripts without CCA were 42 nt (Arg) and 47 nt (Ile) (doi:10.1093/nar/gky593) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6158502/)
- Experimental proof (the only direct evidence found for Enoplea armless tRNAs): several R. culicivorax armless tRNAs are transcribed, processed and given a non-encoded CCA end (5'/3' RACE on Arg and Ile) (doi:10.1016/j.biochi.2013.07.034)
- Armless mt-tRNAs are aminoacylated by a co-evolved CCA-adding enzyme: R. culicivorax mitochondrial CCA-adding enzyme accepts miniaturised armless substrates (doi:10.3390/ijms21239047)
- Annotation artefact on record: for Romanomermis iyengari a published annotation placed tRNA-Ala and tRNA-Cys as superimposed reverse complements at the same locus; with armless CMs credible non-overlapping candidates exist elsewhere in the genome (doi:10.4161/rna.21630)
- Overlaps seen after re-annotation: largest tRNA-tRNA overlap 14 nt (Leu1/His, Thaumamermis cosgrovei), all other tRNA overlaps with tRNAs or PCGs below 10 nt, larger overlaps with rRNA genes in four mermithids, attributed to rRNA boundary uncertainty (doi:10.4161/rna.21630)

### Acari (mites) and Araneae (spiders)
- Dermatophagoides farinae (Acariformes, Astigmata): all 22 tRNAs are minimal, lacking T-arm (TV-replacement loop) or D-arm; length 49-62 nt (mean 54.4), shorter than C. elegans (53-63, mean 56.3, p = 0.019); inferred T-stem only 2-3 bp and thermodynamically unstable; discriminator base probably not encoded (doi:10.1186/1471-2164-10-598) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2797822/)
- Shortest acariform mt-tRNAs reported: 45-49 nt (same paper); in the D. farinae Table 1, seven tRNAs (Arg, Ser1, Ser2, Cys, Tyr, Val, Ala) are D-replacement-loop types and most of the rest TV-replacement-loop; Lys is listed as cloverleaf or TV-loop (my reading of the flattened table) (doi:10.1186/1471-2164-10-598)
- Overlaps in the Dermatophagoides tRNA table: footnotes list a 17 nt overlap with the ND3 3' end, a 12 nt overlap with the 3' end of tRNA-Met, and a tRNA-Tyr overlapping tRNA-Lys by 7 nt and tRNA-Pro by 11 nt; most others overlap a neighbouring gene by 1 nt (doi:10.1186/1471-2164-10-598)
- Steganacarus magnus (Oribatida): original paper reported only six tRNAs (Leu, His, Pro, Trp, Gln, Ser) and 16 lost (doi:10.1186/1471-2164-9-532); a later reanalysis found tRNA-like structures at positions the authors had not annotated (e.g. a tRNA-Lys-like structure at the l-rRNA start "may represent an actual tRNA missed by the authors") (doi:10.1186/1471-2164-10-598) - i.e. at least part of the reported mite tRNA "loss" is detection failure (推测, from these two papers)
- Tetranychus urticae (Trombidiformes): tRNA-seq shows expression, CCA-tailing and base modification of the divergent mt-tRNAs, and also sense AND antisense reads for Arg, Glu, Leu1, Phe, Pro, Ser2, Tyr; antisense-only expression for two genes switches the anticodon/identity relative to the genome annotation (doi:10.1093/nargab/lqaa111) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7803006/)
- Strand/identity ambiguity is documented: the same Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus region is annotated as tRNA-Val or as its antisense complement tRNA-Tyr depending on the study (doi:10.1093/nargab/lqaa111)
- Spiders: Habronattus oregonensis (Araneae) has at least 9 tRNAs with TV-replacement loops and at least 10 tRNAs whose 3' acceptor-stem sequence is absent from the gene (the downstream sequence encodes the next gene), so editing is postulated (doi:10.1093/molbev/msh096)
- Arachnid survey: 8 new genomes, T-arm missing from many of the 22 tRNAs in 4 of 7 orders; all opisthothele spiders have some tRNAs without a well-paired acceptor stem; once T-arm loss occurs it is not regained; some tRNA families lose the arm more readily (doi:10.1093/molbev/msn051)
- MITOS2-based survey shapes: Sarcoptiformes - almost all mt-tRNAs aberrant or not found, Lys the exception; Trombidiformes - Lys and Leu2 mostly cloverleaf, Val and Arg almost absent; Tetranychus - Ile, Ala, Asp, Cys, Pro, Ser2, Thr not annotated; Araneae - cloverleaf versus aberrant calls mixed for almost every family, cloverleaf prevalent for Met, Gln, Leu2; Ixodoidea - Cys lacks an arm except Ixodes; Pseudoscorpiones almost all reduced (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)

### Other lineages (arm lost, which tRNA)
- Ser(AGN) / "Ser1" D-armless is the metazoan default; it is already present in platyhelminth Fasciola hepatica, so it arose at or before the base of Metazoa; the second serine Ser(UCN) is cloverleaf in most Metazoa (doi:10.1007/bf02603072)
- Lophotrochozoa: mt-tRNA-Ser1 lacks an arm in most Annelida and Bivalvia except Mytilidae, cloverleaf in Caenogastropoda, some other gastropods and almost all cephalopods; Ser2 also reduced in Nereididae, Monogenea (Diplozoidae), Digenea; Ser2 commonly D-armless in molluscs (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)
- Arg and Cys arm loss arose repeatedly: Arg in Phyllodocida (polychaetes) and several Eucestoda orders; Cys in Monogenea, Digenea and Eucestoda; Thr in Schistosoma mansoni and some Mytilidae; His in Digenea and Eucestoda; land snails (Stylommatophora) tend to reduced shapes (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)
- Acanthocephala: all annotated mitogenomes lost tRNA-Asn; Eoacanthocephala lost Asp, His, Lys, Thr, Val, Tyr; Pomphorhynchus lost Cys, Asp, Ile, Gln, Arg, Tyr; most retained genes in Palaeacanthocephala lost one arm; Trp stays cloverleaf (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)
- Other arthropods: general pattern is a full cloverleaf set except Ser1 (one-armed); exceptions: Amphipoda (Gln, Arg, Ser1, Ser2, Val aberrant), Isopoda (Ala, Cys truncated, Ile absent), Hemiptera (Cys, Glu, His, Thr, Val), Hymenoptera (Cys, Glu) (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)
- Vertebrates: aberrant shapes rare; Ser1 truncated in Mammalia and many Sauropsida; Cys reduced in some Lepidosauria and in Euphractus sexcinctus (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)
- Global scale (MITOS2 reannotation of 13,387 RefSeq metazoan mitogenomes): about half the genomes (6,288) carry at least one aberrant mt-tRNA; Ser is by far the most affected (4,339 genomes); predicted gene lengths centre around 70 nt but with a broad tail, 458 tRNAs longer than 80 nt in 308 genomes (likely wrong boundaries), and short tRNAs prefer short mitogenomes (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)
- Functional constraint: structurally reduced tRNAs persist over long time scales, so they are probably functional rather than pseudogenes (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232); co-evolution with EF-Tu paralogues in Ecdysozoa and with other interacting factors is the mechanistic explanation proposed (doi:10.4161/rna.21630)

## 2. Quantitative evidence on how existing tools fail on these clades (RefSeq-wide numbers for MiTFi/ARWEN/tRNAscan-SE are in ml_trna_prediction.md and trna_naming_anticodon.md and are not repeated)

### Tool scope, as designed
- tRNAscan-SE 2.0 mt mode is vertebrate-only by design: 22 isotype/anticodon CMs built from vertebrates, plus a D-arm-less Ser(GCU) CM; the authors state they "plan to broaden this capability to mt-tRNA detection in insects, nematodes, fungi" because those clades have mt-tRNAs "missing one or two tRNA arms" and "more diverse model sets will be needed"; the older ad hoc organellar mode "misses mt-tRNAs with highly degenerate secondary structure", e.g. mammalian Ser(GCU) (doi:10.1093/nar/gkab688) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8450103/)
- State of the tRNAscan-SE repository, checked 2026-10-26 with `gh api` tree listing: release v2.0.13 (2026-07-05, only a GCC-15 compile fix per its notes); mitochondrial model files present are `TRNAinf-mito-mammal-*` and `TRNAinf-mito-vert-*` (the vert set includes `Cys-no-darm`); no invertebrate, nematode or arachnid model set exists in master (https://github.com/UCSC-LoweLab/tRNAscan-SE/tree/master/lib/models) (https://github.com/UCSC-LoweLab/tRNAscan-SE/releases/tag/v2.0.13)
- MITOS2 / MiTFi use one cloverleaf CM per family; Ozerova et al. state that "mitos2 uses only cloverleaf-shaped models" so its preference for them "may lead to partially incorrect alignments that incorporate sequences outside of the true mt-tRNA gene"; MITOS2 allows a 50 nt intersection between mt-tRNAs by default (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)
- Jühling 2012 proposed but did not implement an extra MiTFi step: "it may be worthwhile to append a search with specific CMs for aberrant structures as a further step in the MiTFi pipeline"; the nematode-specific results were excluded from their statistical evaluation because they needed significant manual post-processing (doi:10.1093/nar/gkr1131) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3326299)
- ARWEN is a heuristic with three scorers (cloverleaf, D-replacement loop, TV-replacement loop) so a single missing arm is covered by design; no ARWEN measurement on armless Enoplea tRNAs was found (未确证) (doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btm573) (knowledge/tools/arwen.md)
- Pipelines wrapping these callers: MitoFinder uses ARWEN 1.2 for tRNAs (doi:10.1111/1755-0998.13160); MitoZ uses MiTFi with E <= 0.001, `-onlycutoff`, code 2 or 5 (doi:10.1093/nar/gkz173); GeSeq uses BLAT homology to user-chosen references plus optional ARAGORN or tRNAscan-SE (doi:10.1093/nar/gkx391); DeGeCI projects reference annotations by 17-mer matching (doi:10.3389/fgene.2023.1250907). I found no published per-clade tRNA recall for MitoFinder, MitoZ, MitoFlex or GeSeq on armless clades (未确证; Europe PMC and web searches 2026-10-26)

### Clade-level failure evidence (all counts are from the cited papers; "my reading" = counted by me from a flattened table)
- Mites, tRNAscan-SE 2.0 organellar mode with relaxed cut-off (-O -C -X 0.1) detected only 4 of the annotated Tetranychus urticae mt-tRNAs (Asn, His, Leu2, Lys); "all other tRNAs were not detected by tRNAscan-SE"; the authors state that tRNAscan-SE and ARWEN "frequently fail to detect the highly degenerate mt-tRNAs in Acariformes" (doi:10.1093/nargab/lqaa111)
- Mites, Dermatophagoides farinae Table 1 (22 tRNAs, tRNAscan-SE 1.x and ARWEN 1.2 era): my reading is tRNAscan-SE returned no tRNA for 6 of 22 (Arg, Ser1, Ser2, Tyr, Val, Ala) and ARWEN none for 3 of 22 (Met, Val, Ile); only Val was missed by both; "unambiguous" scores for 12 (tRNAscan-SE) and 11 (ARWEN) of 22; the same ARWEN run gave a cloverleaf in one Dermatophagoides species and a D-loop type in the other for the same locus (doi:10.1186/1471-2164-10-598)
- Mites, oribatid Oribatula sakamorii: tRNAscan-SE + ARWEN + MITOS + MITOS2 combined found 21 of 22; trnV needed manual alignment with homologous species using anticodon and secondary structure; the same re-annotation recovered every tRNA reported "lost" in Steganacarus magnus and Paraleius leontonychus, so the earlier loss claims were detection failures; the authors conclude that the atypical structures "made them unidentifiable using a single type of tRNA search program" (doi:10.1186/s13071-021-04719-0) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8063316/)
- Mites, Steganacarus magnus tRNA count moved from 6 (original, 16 "lost") to 12 after reannotation with MITOS2 and other callers (doi:10.1038/s41598-018-25981-w) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5954100/); Zhan 2021 then reports the full 22 for another oribatid, Oribatula sakamorii (doi:10.1186/s13071-021-04719-0)
- Acanthocephala, Heterosentis pseudobagri: MITOS and ARWEN could not identify trnH, trnQ, trnM, trnD, trnN, trnI, trnR; they were annotated manually by comparing with orthologues, in some cases only from the conserved central segment around the anticodon because the 5' and 3' flanks had no similarity to orthologues; ARWEN folded one gene into a cloverleaf but called it trnI instead of trnT; assembling the mitogenome from transcriptome data excluded sequencing artefacts (doi:10.1186/s12864-023-09177-9) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9979467/)
- Scale insects: MITOS web server found only 13 tRNAs in Ceroplastes japonicus (doi:10.1080/23802359.2019.1622464); in Saissetia coffeae nine tRNAs were "not found using MITOS", most detected tRNAs lacked the D- or T-arm, and the nine were recovered by a Coccidae-specific workflow that tolerates mismatched base pairs (doi:10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2020.04.257)
- Amphipoda: amphipod-specific MiTFi models revealed 17 tRNA duplications (10 remolded) that Metazoa-wide models and earlier annotations overlooked (doi:10.1016/j.ympev.2019.106710)
- Nematoda/Enoplea, MITOS2 (Ozerova): "lacks sensitivity for such extreme cases and, at the same time, tends to extend predictions into the surroundings"; custom truncated CMs (T-armless, D-armless, armless variants built by deleting arms from the MITOS2 alignments) raised sensitivity and add armless shapes but "shapes with D-arm loss are still not detected" and they are "not sufficiently sensitive to recognize all mt-tRNAs"; CMs built from the hand-curated Jühling 2012 Mermithidae alignments retrieve an almost complete set, which the authors note is expected because the training sequences are close relatives (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)
- Nematoda, tRNAscan-SE nematode setting finds fewer than half of nematode mt-tRNA genes (already in trna_naming_anticodon.md, Morrison preprint) (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.3813)
- MiTFi false-positive structure (Jühling 2012, over RefSeq v39): of about 3,250 hits inspected manually, 272 apparent false positives in 170 organisms were true tRNAs (about 30 from Metatheria due to anticodon assignment, 242 new); remaining false positives are mostly forced best hits for tRNAs a clade lacks: Cnidaria about 283, Arthropoda 71, Nematoda 31 (doi:10.1093/nar/gkr1131)
- Genome-scale hint of residual error in the standard tool: in the MITOS2 reannotation, tRNA length distribution has a long tail (458 tRNAs > 80 nt in 308 genomes), and the authors regard these as inaccurate predictions (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)

## 3. Other pain points beyond arm loss

### 3a. Post-transcriptional repair: genomic acceptor stems that do not pair, tRNA ends inside the next gene
- Centipede Lithobius forficatus: only 1 of 22 tRNA genes encodes a fully paired acceptor stem; the other 21 have up to 5 mismatches and some overlap the downstream gene; cDNA of 8 tRNAs showed editing of up to 5 nt at the 3' end, using the 5' half of the acceptor stem as template (doi:10.1073/pnas.250402997) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC17645/)
- Velvet worms (Onychophora, Oroperipatus sp. and Peripatoides sympatrica): tRNA genes are highly truncated and "restored to more conventional structures" by editing; up to 34 nt are added in Oroperipatus, rebuilding acceptor stem, TΨC arm, in extreme cases variable arm and part of the anticodon stem; cDNA from 14 and 10 tRNAs; present in both extant families for more than 140 My; the authors suggest similar editing may occur in other arthropod groups with strong tRNA reduction (doi:10.1093/molbev/msr113)
- Land snails: acceptor-stem mismatches fall mostly where the tRNA gene overlaps the downstream gene; cDNA of four circularised tRNAs differed from the genes at 13 positions, all in the 3' half of the acceptor stem and the discriminator, mostly toward A, restoring pairing; polyadenylation proposed as an intermediate (doi:10.1073/pnas.92.22.10432) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC40811/)
- Spiders (Habronattus oregonensis): at least 10 tRNAs lack genomic 3' acceptor-stem sequence because the downstream sequence encodes the next gene; editing postulated (doi:10.1093/molbev/msh096); arachnid survey: all opisthothele spiders have some unpaired acceptor stems (doi:10.1093/molbev/msn051)
- Mites: the discriminator base is probably not encoded in many Dermatophagoides tRNAs and is added posttranscriptionally (doi:10.1186/1471-2164-10-598); Tetranychus mt-tRNAs carry non-encoded CCA (doi:10.1093/nargab/lqaa111)
- Sponges: Clathrina clathrus (Calcarea) 24 tRNAs with multiple acceptor-stem mismatches repaired by 3'-end editing (doi:10.1093/molbev/mss274)
- Consequence for an annotator (推测, from the above): a covariance model that requires a fully paired acceptor stem will either miss the gene or place its 3' end wrongly; the 5' half of the acceptor stem plus the anticodon stem-loop are the stable anchors, and the 3' end of the gene should be allowed to run into the next gene (several nt, up to the 34 nt seen in Onychophora) (doi:10.1093/molbev/msr113)

### 3b. tRNA boundaries, overlaps with PCGs and with each other
- tRNA punctuation model: mt tRNA excision defines mRNA and rRNA ends; in D. melanogaster RACE/circularised RT-PCR on all major transcripts showed the model predicts 3' ends of most mRNAs and rRNAs, non-coding residues at 5' ends are removed, and clustered tRNAs (e.g. between nad3 and nad5) are removed sequentially 3' to 5' (doi:10.1016/j.gene.2009.06.006) (original model doi:10.1038/290470a0)
- Compact genomes break the "no overlap" assumption: in the isopod Armadillidium vulgare (13.9 kb, apparently incomplete tRNA set) expression of 13 tRNA genes was confirmed, including tRNAs in large gene overlaps and tRNA genes partially or fully inside protein-coding genes in direct or opposite orientation, plus extensive repair of a truncated tRNA (doi:10.1080/15476286.2015.1090078)
- Sense and antisense pairs: in Tetranychus both strands are expressed for Arg, Glu, Leu1, Phe, Pro, Ser2, Tyr; two Drosophila melanogaster antisense tRNAs (ThrAS, ProAS) carry 3' CCA (doi:10.1093/nargab/lqaa111) (Drosophila: preprint doi:10.1101/2021.06.05.447193, published as doi:10.1016/j.mito.2022.06.006; the CCA statement was read from the preprint only)
- Antisense claims can be annotation artefacts: in Enoplea, near-complete opposite-strand overlaps proposed for Romanomermis were replaced by non-overlapping armless candidates (doi:10.4161/rna.21630)
- Unresolved: whether strand/identity "switches" in mites (antisense-only expression) should change the annotated feature is not settled by the authors (doi:10.1093/nargab/lqaa111)
- tRNA genes with 5' end overlap into the preceding PCG in mites reach 17 nt (ND3) and 12 nt (tRNA-Met) in Dermatophagoides (doi:10.1186/1471-2164-10-598); 14 nt tRNA-tRNA overlap (Leu1/His) in Thaumamermis (doi:10.4161/rna.21630); overlaps above 10 nt deserve a flag, but are not necessarily wrong (推测)

### 3c. Anticodon reading, Leu/Ser identity, genetic-code dependence
- Ser(AGN) anticodon is not stable across Metazoa: in arthropods AGG is Lys in many lineages and "point mutations at tRNA-Lys and tRNA-Ser correlated with the meaning of the AGG codon"; two alternative codes occur within many taxa (doi:10.3109/19401736.2011.653801)
- In the MITOS2 reannotation of 13,387 RefSeq genomes (run with the vertebrate code as default), Ser1 is "frequently reported as missing while, at the same time, an mt-tRNA-X is annotated" in Ecdysozoa and Lophotrochozoa, where X = anticodon that is a stop in the code used; the authors assume an anticodon change tied to a variant code; whether the X calls are real anticodon changes or code-selection artefacts was not tested (未确证) (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)
- The tRNA identity can be right while the anticodon-derived name is wrong: ARWEN folded a Heterosentis tRNA-Thr into a cloverleaf but called it trnI because the divergent 3' end misled it (doi:10.1186/s12864-023-09177-9)
- Remolding (duplicate gene + anticodon mutation + loss of the ancestral gene) changes the identity of a tRNA so that its sequence is closer to another family; methods to detect it in large datasets exist and found "several novel remolding events"; remolding "can lead to artifacts in the annotation of mitogenomes" (doi:10.1093/nar/gkv746)
- Amphipods: 10 of 17 detected tRNA duplications are remolded; seven of ten occur in Lake Baikal endemics; an R script in the companion Data Brief diagnoses the ancestral tRNA specificity independent of the anticodon using genetic distances (doi:10.1016/j.ympev.2019.106710) (doi:10.1016/j.dib.2020.105284)
- MFannot predicts tRNA identity from the anticodon and a user-supplied genetic code and "will err in rare cases of codon reassignment" because it does not use identity elements such as acceptor-stem G3:U70 (doi:10.3389/fpls.2023.1222186)
- Leu1/Leu2 and Ser1/Ser2 numbering conflicts are in trna_naming_anticodon.md; here only the added point that naming by anticodon + code is more robust than naming by CM family (doi:10.1093/nar/gkr1131)

### 3d. Duplicates, pseudogenes, rearrangement
- Duplicated or extra tRNAs: MITOS2 annotation lists a duplicated Thr and a missing Arg in the tunicate Herdmania; "X in addition to the usual 22" occurs in some clades (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232); aberrant tRNAs that persist over long times are probably functional because tandem-duplication-random-loss clears pseudogenes quickly (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)
- Louse minichromosomes and tRNA pseudogenes, mussel 23rd tRNA, mite order changes, and triclad tRNA translocations are in taxon_specific_mtdna.md and trna_naming_anticodon.md and are not repeated (knowledge/topics/taxon_specific_mtdna.md:96-139) (knowledge/topics/trna_naming_anticodon.md)
- Rearrangement undermines synteny-based tRNA assignment: tRNAs are the most mobile elements of arthropod mtDNA (doi:10.1186/s12915-021-01218-7) (via taxon_specific_mtdna.md:138); Enoplea armless-tRNA sequence elements persist across rearrangements that move them between different flanking genes, so sequence similarity, not position, is the reliable identity cue (doi:10.4161/rna.21630)

### 3e. Taxa with few tRNAs (additions to trna_naming_anticodon.md)
- Acanthocephala (ongoing loss): Asn lost in all annotated genomes; Eoacanthocephala lack Asp, His, Lys, Thr, Val, Tyr; Pomphorhynchus lacks Cys, Asp, Ile, Gln, Arg, Tyr; Trp always present as cloverleaf (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)
- Mites: Sarcoptiformes and Trombidiformes have patchy sets in annotations, but several "losses" were later found (see section 2), so absence in a mite or acanthocephalan record should be treated as 未确证 unless the clade is known to have lost the gene (doi:10.1186/s13071-021-04719-0) (doi:10.1186/s12864-023-09177-9)
- Where losses are real, missing mt-tRNAs are compensated by import of cytosolic tRNAs (stated for Cnidaria and Chaetognatha by Jühling; import in general by Ozerova) (doi:10.1093/nar/gkr1131) (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)

## 4. Methods and datasets from 2019-2026 (and what is still missing)

### 4a. Published approaches that actually improved aberrant-tRNA recovery
- Arm-truncated CM set + armless CMs (Ozerova 2024): starting from the alignments behind the MITOS2 CMs, delete the loops, or the whole arms, of D and/or T to get T-less, D-less and armless variants; add Enoplea CMs built with cmbuild/cmcalibrate from the Jühling 2012 alignments; search with cmsearch in global mode (`-g`) with all HMM filters off (`--max`); accept only hits whose anticodon is correct and left unpaired; define the anticodon loop as the central loop (short models) or a loop with at least 7 or 9 unpaired bases (long models); group hits by anticodon-loop locus and strand, keep the best bit-score per locus with no threshold, allow near-equal duplicates, then curate manually; result: more sensitive than MITOS2 for armless shapes, D-loss still missed, not complete without manual work (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)
- Clade-specific CM seeds beat Metazoa-wide models: amphipod-specific MiTFi models (Romanova 2020, 17 duplications, 10 remolded); nematode/Enoplea CMs (Jühling 2012); hand-curated Mermithidae alignments retrieve an almost complete set in Romanomermis (doi:10.1016/j.ympev.2019.106710) (doi:10.4161/rna.21630) (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)
- Conserved-position comparative folding (Pons 2019, six Dysderidae spiders, 32 further spider mitogenomes for gene-order support): the accurate fold of the four armless tRNAs (A, R, S1, Y) "could only be achieved manually thanks to the highly conserved nucleotide sequences across the species, particularly the anticodon arm"; inconsistent presence or absence of a D or T arm between close relatives was "a crucial hint" for armless shape; MiTFi inside MITOS2 recovered only 2 armless tRNAs (A in P. romandiolae, S1 in P. riberai; 4 armless families x 6 species = 24 instances, my arithmetic) and no cutoff (COVE < -50 in tRNAscan-SE, loose Infernal E-values) fixed this (doi:10.1186/s12864-019-6026-1) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6706885/)
- Multi-tool union + manual homology rescue (Zhan 2021, oribatid mites): tRNAscan-SE + ARWEN + MITOS + MITOS2 combined recovered 21/22; the last (trnV) needed alignment with homologous species; RNAfold minimum free energy was used for constrained folds (doi:10.1186/s13071-021-04719-0)
- Reference-projection annotation (DeGeCI, 2023; Leipzig group): projects RefSeq annotations of matching genomes by 17-mer lookups in a de Bruijn graph, treats tRNAs like any other gene; on a 100-genome RefSeq 89 sample (own genome excluded; truth = RefSeq) it reproduced 2,141 of 2,162 tRNAs (99.0%; 3 FP, 9 FN) against MITOS2 2,134 (98.7%; 0 FP, 13 FN); the paper reports 96% of tRNA starts/stops within 5 nt of RefSeq; no armless-clade test is reported (doi:10.3389/fgene.2023.1250907) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10448254/)
- tRNAscan-SE 2.0 (2021): vertebrate mt CMs only; extension to insects and nematodes announced as future work and not present in the repository tree as of 2026-10-26 (doi:10.1093/nar/gkab688) (https://github.com/UCSC-LoweLab/tRNAscan-SE)
- Remolding detection (Sahyoun 2015) and amphipod R scripts (Romanova 2020): tree/distance-based methods to assign the ancestral identity of duplicated tRNAs independent of the anticodon (doi:10.1093/nar/gkv746) (doi:10.1016/j.dib.2020.105284)
- ML/DL: no mt-tRNA-specific deep-learning gene finder or organelle-annotation language model found; extra Europe PMC title queries on 2026-10-26 (tRNA + mitochondrial + predict/detect/identify/annotate, 2019-2026) returned six hits, none a method (未确证, search-based); see ml_trna_prediction.md for the earlier negative result

### 4b. Experimentally supported invertebrate mt-tRNA sets usable as independent truth (not derived from MITOS/MiTFi output)
- Tetranychus urticae (spider mite): tRNA-seq reads covering all previously annotated mt-tRNA regions, CCA tails and modifications, plus sense and antisense reads; raw reads SRA BioProject PRJNA662108, processed counts GEO GSE162913; reads mapped to RefSeq mt genome NC_010526 and seven other Tetranychus accessions; caveat: reads came from an infested plant sample, species/strain assigned by mapping (doi:10.1093/nargab/lqaa111) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7803006/)
- Drosophila melanogaster, D. simulans, D. grimshawi, Bactrocera oleae: pan-RNA-seq reannotation corrected five D. melanogaster tRNAs (Arg, Asn, Thr, Pro, Val) in RefSeq NC_024511.2, found CCA on two antisense tRNAs, and provides D. simulans MN611461 as improved reference; dataset accession list (SRP017617, SRP199841, ERP011832, ERP012885, ERP106913 sRNA-seq; SRP302633 and ERP117816 cDNA-seq; ERP110987 D. simulans; SRP135764 D. grimshawi; SRP189010 B. oleae) was extracted by the page-fetch tool from the preprint and must be checked against the paper before use (doi:10.1016/j.mito.2022.06.006) (https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.06.05.447193v1.full)
- Drosophila melanogaster mature transcript ends (5'/3' RACE, circularised RT-PCR) for all major mt transcripts give experimentally defined tRNA/PCG junctions (doi:10.1016/j.gene.2009.06.006)
- Romanomermis culicivorax: 5'/3' RACE of mt-tRNA-Arg and -Ile and other armless tRNAs, non-encoded CCA (doi:10.1016/j.biochi.2013.07.034); NMR/SAXS structures of the two (doi:10.1093/nar/gky593); raw reads or accession numbers for the RACE clones 未确证
- Armadillidium vulgare: expression of 13 tRNA genes confirmed by RT-PCR including tRNAs inside PCGs and a tRNA-His with six acceptor-stem mismatches repaired by nucleotide replacement plus extra CCA and G-1 (doi:10.1080/15476286.2015.1090078); the tRNA-His details are quoted in doi:10.1186/s12864-019-6026-1
- Editing sets (cDNA): Lithobius forficatus 8 tRNAs (doi:10.1073/pnas.250402997); Onychophora 14 + 10 tRNAs (doi:10.1093/molbev/msr113); land snails 4 tRNAs (doi:10.1073/pnas.92.22.10432); GenBank accession numbers of the cDNA clones 未确证
- Acanthocephala: mitogenome assembled from transcriptome data of Heterosentis pseudobagri to exclude sequencing artefacts for anticodon-core-only tRNAs (doi:10.1186/s12864-023-09177-9)
- Functional assays: Trichinella britovi EF-Tu1 binds cloverleaf, T-armless and D-armless tRNAs (an "intermediate state"), Chromadorea EF-Tu1 only T-armless (doi:10.1093/nar/gkl526); R. culicivorax CCA-adding enzyme accepts armless substrates (doi:10.3390/ijms21239047)
- Size of the usable truth: these are tens of tRNAs in about 6-8 species; no genome-wide curated armless-tRNA truth set exists beyond the manually curated shape tables of Ozerova 2024 (Zenodo 13789952, derived from MITOS2 plus manual curation) and Jühling 2012 supplementary alignments (未确证 for completeness)

## 5. Benchmark design: how truth was built and how circular it is

- Jühling 2012: truth = RefSeq v39 (40,521 tRNAs, 1,876 genomes); but the seed alignments for MiTFi were built from tRNAscan-SE 1.23 (-O -X 5) and ARWEN 1.2.3 scans of the same RefSeq v39 genomes and then "extensive manual editing"; therefore agreement with RefSeq partly measures consistency of related tool families; the authors say RefSeq "is not a perfect gold standard" (7 of 16 echinoderm mitogenomes with errors), so they inspected about 3,250 false-positive hits and reclassified 272 (170 organisms) as true tRNAs; the nematode armless results were kept out of the statistics (doi:10.1093/nar/gkr1131) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3326299)
- tRNAscan-SE 2.0 (2021): truth = RefSeq annotations of 3,345 vertebrate mitogenomes; five outcome classes - consistent (within 15 nt of both ends and same isotype), isotype mismatch, position mismatch (strand or beyond +/-15 nt), novel, not detected; non-matching predictions were inspected by hand; CMs were trained on mt-tRNAs of 1,085 vertebrate species from mitotRNAdb, and whether the 3,345-genome test set excludes those training species is not stated (未确证); negative control = 17,500 virtual mt genomes from a fifth-order Markov chain keeping base composition of five genomes (3,500 each) (doi:10.1093/nar/gkab688) (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8450103/)
- Ozerova 2024: labels = MITOS2 output on 13,387 RefSeq genomes (vertebrate code), then shapes curated by hand for selected clades; validation of armless shapes only for Romanomermis (experimental data) and indirectly via literature; the authors state that most reported structures "are based on comparative sequence analysis only" so gene boundaries remain uncertain (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232)
- DeGeCI 2023: truth = RefSeq 89 annotation of 100 sampled genomes; inconsistent Leu/Ser anticodon naming in RefSeq is explicitly named as a source of "false" mismatches (doi:10.3389/fgene.2023.1250907)
- RefSeq itself was partly generated or checked with tRNAscan-SE during NCBI's organelle clean-up (3,679 tRNA additions etc.; organelle share not given), so tool-vs-RefSeq agreement for tRNAscan-SE is not independent (doi:10.1093/nar/gkn721) (already in trna_naming_anticodon.md)
- No benchmark found that (a) stratifies tRNA recall by shape (cloverleaf / D-less / T-less / armless), (b) splits train and test by family or order, or (c) uses transcript-supported truth for a non-model invertebrate set; 未确证 (absence from the papers read, Europe PMC and web searches 2026-10-26)

## Actionable for MitoM

Each item is a concrete algorithm change; "evidence" gives the source, "caveat" what is not proven. MitoM keeps MITOS2/MiTFi as the primary caller (see ml_trna_prediction.md); these items sit around it.

1. Second-pass shape-aware CM search for tRNAs MITOS2 did not place. Build T-less, D-less and armless CMs by deleting arm loops/arms from the same alignments behind the MITOS2 family CMs; run `cmsearch -g --max` on the whole sequence (or only on unannotated gaps plus a margin); keep a hit only if the anticodon matches the expected family and sits unpaired in the anticodon loop; group hits by anticodon-loop locus and strand and keep the highest bit-score per locus; label the shape (cloverleaf / D-less / T-less / armless). Evidence: Ozerova 2024 Methods (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232); Jühling 2012 proposal to append aberrant-structure CMs to the MiTFi pipeline (doi:10.1093/nar/gkr1131). Caveat: D-arm-loss shapes were still not detected by their truncated CMs, so also add D-less models seeded from the Ser1 alignments (the one D-less family present in the MITOS2 data) 推测.
2. Taxon-routed clade-specific CM packs, switched on by NCBI taxonomy of the input: Enoplea (Mermithida, Dorylaimida, Trichocephalida) from the Jühling 2012 alignments (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.13789952 also ships nematode CMs); acariform mites and spiders from curated alignments in Klimov & OConnor 2009, Schäffer 2018, Pons 2019, Zhan 2021; amphipods from Romanova 2020; Acanthocephala from Gao 2023. Evidence: Mermithidae-based CMs retrieve an almost complete Romanomermis set (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232); amphipod models found 17 duplications missed by Metazoa-wide models (doi:10.1016/j.ympev.2019.106710); Pons 2019 and Zhan 2021 show close-relative alignments are what resolves the armless/atypical cases (doi:10.1186/s12864-019-6026-1) (doi:10.1186/s13071-021-04719-0). Caveat: models trained on close relatives overfit those relatives; the Mermithidae result is "not surprising" by the authors' own words, so test on held-out families.
3. Anchor on the anticodon stem-loop and the 5' half of the acceptor stem, and let the gene's 3' end run into the next gene. Do not require a fully paired acceptor stem: allow several mismatches (observed up to 5-6 in Lithobius, Armadillidium, spiders) when the anticodon core and the 5' acceptor half match a same-clade homologue, and allow the 3' end to overlap the downstream gene by several nt (observed up to 17 nt with ND3 in Dermatophagoides; editing adds up to 34 nt in Onychophora). Evidence: doi:10.1073/pnas.250402997; doi:10.1093/molbev/msr113; doi:10.1073/pnas.92.22.10432; doi:10.1093/molbev/msh096; doi:10.1186/1471-2164-10-598; Pons 2019 states that loose COVE/Infernal cutoffs did not rescue mismatched armless spider tRNAs (doi:10.1186/s12864-019-6026-1). Caveat: edited 3' ends mean the "true" end in the genome is a convention; record an `acceptor_mismatch` flag rather than invent a boundary.
4. Orthologue-core rescue for any family still missing after steps 1-3: take the 17-25 nt anticodon-arm core of the same family from the nearest annotated relatives (same order/family in RefSeq), align it to intergenic gaps and to the ends of flanking genes, then extend to the conserved flank. Evidence: Heterosentis tRNAs annotated "only on the basis of the conserved narrow central segment comprising the anticodon" (doi:10.1186/s12864-023-09177-9); Oribatula trnV found by alignment with homologous species (doi:10.1186/s13071-021-04719-0); anticodon loop is nearly absolutely conserved across Enoplea for all 22 families (doi:10.4161/rna.21630). Caveat: a core hit without a foldable flank is lower-confidence; report it as `core_only`.
5. Never force 22 tRNAs and never accept "lost" in mites/Acanthocephala/Nematoda without the gap scan of steps 1-4. Expected counts by clade should be a property of the clade (Cnidaria 2, Ctenophora 0, etc., see trna_naming_anticodon.md), not a global 22. Evidence: forced best hits cause about 283 Cnidaria, 71 Arthropoda and 31 Nematoda false positives in MiTFi (doi:10.1093/nar/gkr1131); S. magnus "16 lost" became 12 and then a full set in a relative, and the "lost" Paraleius tRNAs were found (doi:10.1038/s41598-018-25981-w) (doi:10.1186/s13071-021-04719-0).
6. Overlap and boundary policy with flags, not silent trimming: accept tRNA-tRNA and tRNA-PCG overlaps below 10 nt as normal; flag 10-20 nt as `long_overlap`; allow antisense overlap only if the antisense copy has independent support (conserved homologue in relatives or RNA-seq); do not let a PCG call swallow a whole tRNA (the existing MitoZ failure in mitfi.md:45). Evidence: Enoplea reannotation kept all overlaps below 10 nt except one 14 nt tRNA-tRNA case (doi:10.4161/rna.21630); mite overlaps of 12-17 nt (doi:10.1186/1471-2164-10-598); tRNA punctuation model predicts mRNA/rRNA ends from tRNA ends (doi:10.1016/j.gene.2009.06.006); antisense proposals in Romanomermis disappeared with armless CMs (doi:10.4161/rna.21630). Caveat: Armadillidium has genuine tRNA-inside-PCG cases (doi:10.1080/15476286.2015.1090078), so flag rather than delete.
7. Name each tRNA from anticodon plus an explicitly passed genetic code, then reconcile Ser/Leu families with a rule set: if a Ser1-like D-less gene is present but its anticodon decodes a stop in the chosen code, re-test with the clade's actual code before calling it `trnX`; keep L1/L2 and S1/S2 as sequence-class labels, not as anticodon proxies. Check duplicated families for remolding with a distance/tree test before keeping the new identity. Evidence: Abascal 2012 (AGG = Lys in many arthropods; tRNA-Ser and tRNA-Lys point mutations track the code) (doi:10.3109/19401736.2011.653801); Ozerova 2024 `mt-tRNA-X` pattern under the vertebrate default code (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232); remolding artefacts (doi:10.1093/nar/gkv746); ARWEN calling a tRNA-Thr trnI (doi:10.1186/s12864-023-09177-9). Caveat: whether Ozerova's X calls are code artefacts is not tested (未确证).
8. Strand check for sense/antisense ambiguity in mites: when a candidate and its reverse complement both fold (e.g. Val vs Tyr in D. pteronyssinus), keep both as candidates, prefer the strand supported by a same-clade homologue and by neighbouring gene orientation, and mark `strand_ambiguous`. Evidence: Tetranychus antisense-only expression flips the identity of two genes relative to the annotation (doi:10.1093/nargab/lqaa111).
9. Emit shape and confidence metadata per tRNA (shape, acceptor_mismatch, core_only, long_overlap, strand_ambiguous, source = MITOS2 / shape-CM / orthologue-core) for QC and for the review step, and keep these out of the NCBI feature table (they are QC, not submission fields; the NCBI limit for the tRNA key is 50-150 bases, see trna_naming_anticodon.md). Evidence: shape classification of Ozerova 2024 (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232); minimal armless tRNAs are 42-47 nt without CCA and 45 nt with CCA, so a lower length filter of 50 nt would drop real armless genes (doi:10.1093/nar/gky593) (doi:10.1093/gbe/evae232). Caveat: whether table2asn/NCBI accept sub-50 nt tRNA features is not tested here (未确证); check before submission.
10. Evaluate against independent truth, stratified by shape and clade, with family-level holdout. Use: Tetranychus tRNA-seq (PRJNA662108, GSE162913), Drosophila RNA-seq reannotation, Romanomermis RACE set, Armadillidium RT-PCR set, plus clade-curated alignments (Jühling 2012 Enoplea, Pons 2019 spiders); negatives from Markov-chain virtual genomes (tRNAscan-SE 2.0 design); scoring classes as in tRNAscan-SE 2.0 (consistent within 15 nt and same isotype / isotype mismatch / position mismatch / novel / not detected). Report recall for cloverleaf vs D-less vs T-less vs armless separately. Evidence: doi:10.1093/nargab/lqaa111; doi:10.1016/j.mito.2022.06.006; doi:10.1016/j.biochi.2013.07.034; doi:10.1080/15476286.2015.1090078; doi:10.1093/nar/gkab688. Caveat: the usable truth is small (about 6-8 species); RefSeq or MITOS2 labels must not be the only truth because they are partly derived from the same tools (doi:10.1093/nar/gkr1131) (doi:10.1093/nar/gkn721).
